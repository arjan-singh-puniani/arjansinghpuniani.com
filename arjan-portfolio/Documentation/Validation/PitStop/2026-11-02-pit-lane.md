# Pit Stop Lab: exterior pit box and crew choreography

Race service now takes place on an exterior apron beside a separate fast lane, outside the garage. Indoor inspection remains available. The shared analytic path controls both car position/heading and the directed entry/rejoin camera. The full operation plays at 1× entry/exit and 0.35× stationary service. This is schematic geometry, consistent with [F1’s definition of a pit box](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/f1-glossary-a-e.1MFONigMlQSbSQtpP7YCy2), not surveyed Bahrain.

Handlers now face their own removal/replacement lanes. Tires are staged before arrival. Removed wheels clear the hub before moving forward and lifting; replacements move inward before crossing to the axle, avoiding the gunner. The rear jack stages outside the arrival corridor and both operators step aside for departure. Crew rise before retreating. Compact foot contacts, fixed limb lengths, body weight transfer and eased toe-off/landing replace stretched limbs and flat sliding feet.

The service camera moved outside the handler silhouette. Race follow cameras interpolate offsets relative to the moving car; directed shots dwell at least five source seconds, including an uninterrupted cockpit segment. The primary glow trail was removed, avoiding 100 historical pose samples per default frame. Both renderers share a 1.6 million pixel drawing budget and 1.5× scale ceiling.

- Targeted ESLint with `--no-ignore` passes. Next production build passes.
- Browser reports accompany this file: cinema, world, wheel alignment, crew and venue journeys; no application exceptions in successful runs.
- Clean-stop rendered scan: no measured leg/tire or helmet overlap; support-foot drift below 1e-14 m; arms retain 0.31/0.30 m segment lengths; outgoing tire grips remain within 0.425 m of tire center. Capsule/helmet clearances are conservative samples, not a full-body collision proof.
- Entire 65.8 s lap → pit → lap recording inspected in timestamped frames; a separate slow crew sequence was inspected at 0.25 s recording intervals. Mobile WebKit portrait views cover arrival, service, wheel exchange, release and departure. Recordings and full reports are preserved in the local `pit-lane-2026-11-02` evidence directory.

Retina sample on Apple M2, 1440 × 943 CSS pixels at DPR 2: both drawing buffers were 1563 × 1023, down from approximately 3.05/3.47 million pixels to 1.60 million. Four-second samples measured 59.35–60.16 rendered fps across Chrome Metal and WebKit, with no draw gap above 33.8 ms. These short local samples are not a physical-mobile or all-device guarantee.

## Limits

Procedural choreography is not motion capture, a contact-dynamics simulation or a universal collision solver. Mobile emulation does not establish physical-phone performance. Manual screen-reader review and professional pit-crew validation were not performed. Archived telemetry, historical ML and conditional strategy comparisons retain their existing provenance and uncertainty limits.
