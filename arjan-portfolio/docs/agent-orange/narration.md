# Narration draft

Target runtime: approximately 105 seconds at an estimated 122 words per minute, including pauses. This is a script estimate; it has not been timed against a recorded read or synchronized to picture.

## 00:00–00:08 · The long shadow

A war ends on a calendar. Exposure can leave a longer question behind.

## 00:08–00:20 · What was sprayed

Agent Orange contained two herbicides: 2,4-D and 2,4,5-T. TCDD was a contaminant formed during 2,4,5-T manufacture. These are different exposures.

## 00:20–00:35 · The brain

Parkinson disease involves loss of dopamine-producing neurons in the substantia nigra and changes in the connected nigrostriatal system. This schematic is not tissue from the veterans in the study.

## 00:35–00:52 · The cell

A neuron carries signals through a cell body, branching dendrites, an axon, and terminals. Cellular detail can help us see what researchers study. It cannot, by itself, identify what caused disease in a person.

## 00:52–01:10 · What remains unknown

Environmental exposures are studied through many kinds of evidence. Laboratory mechanisms can suggest questions. They do not establish that a specific pathway occurred in this cohort. Association, mechanism, and causation require different evidence.

## 01:10–01:25 · A connected system

When dopamine-producing neurons are lost, signaling through the nigrostriatal pathway changes. Parkinson disease affects movement in complex ways. A population study of diagnoses does not directly measure this biology in each participant.

## 01:25–01:45 · The study

Among 4,468,509 US veterans, reported Agent Orange exposure was associated with higher Parkinson disease diagnosis rates: adjusted hazard ratio 1.44. The direct or occupational subgroup estimate was 1.99. These are relative estimates, not personal probabilities. The study did not isolate 2,4-D or prove causation.
