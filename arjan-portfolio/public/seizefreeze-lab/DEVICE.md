# Device construction: evidence, geometry and unresolved engineering

Engineering review, 1 November 2026. The rendered assembly is an **assumed educational demonstrator of SeizeFreeze's proposed architecture**, not validated implant CAD, a selected commercial thermoelectric module, a manufacturing drawing, or a tested device. Correct construction topology improves the explanation; it does not establish implant feasibility.

The project [architecture illustration](/images/neurotechnology/seizefreeze/biological-effects.webp) and [packaging illustration](/images/neurotechnology/seizefreeze/thermoelectric-cooling.webp) support the component families: sensing, contact, cold spreader, thermoelectric element, hot-side path, control/power and candidate packaging. They do not establish dimensions, materials qualification, complete electrical routing, contact mechanics, hermeticity, heat rejection or chronic operation. Their proposed alternatives must remain alternatives. The supplied anatomical cutaway/segmentation advertisements are visual references, not scientific evidence.

## Concrete defects in the audited baseline and minimum corrections

These findings describe the scene inspected before the construction revision; they are not claims that a later revision still contains the same defects.

| Audited baseline | Why it misteaches the system | Minimum defensible correction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Forty semiconductor legs were joined only by twenty lower-face links. Upper links and a complete pair of module terminals were absent. | This shows disconnected pairs rather than one electrically series-connected module. Decorative traces cannot carry the illustrated command through the array. | Show a single continuous alternating P/N series path using isolated patterned interconnects on both inner substrate faces, including row transitions and two terminals. All legs share the cold/hot faces thermally. The illustrative couple count is not a SeizeFreeze specification. |
| A flat 10 × 10 mm interface was placed 0.65 mm above one cortical tangent point. A separate conformal patch followed the folds. | A tangent-plane placement does not guarantee clearance over a finite footprint. The rigid plate and surface patch were not joined by a defined contact layer. | Sample the entire rigid footprint, establish clearance above the underlying reference mesh, and show the intervening conformal layer or explicitly unresolved gap. Never silently flatten or penetrate the brain to seat a component. Clearance is a geometry check, not evidence of appropriate contact pressure. |
| The heat-path metal ended as a short free stub. | A conductor without a defined thermal destination does not explain the solver's finite connection to an external reservoir. | End the explanatory path at a clearly marked model boundary. Distinguish a hypothetical implanted rejection route from an external bench sink. Do not imply that a tiny stub disposes of heat or solves chronic rejection. |
| A lid and two side rails surrounded an exposed board and open TEC. Leads crossed no defined sealed boundary. | A metal appearance or fastener is not an enclosure, insulated feedthrough, or hermetic seal. | Use a complete candidate enclosure with an explicitly removed wall for the interior view, a candidate closure seam and isolated feedthrough locations. The clearance defect was checked directly from the baseline GLB and mounting transform. The target was approximately `[-63.618, −1.806, 10.481] mm`, with outward normal `[-0.95893, −0.09750, 0.26637]`. An atlas vertex inside the rotated 10 × 10 mm footprint stood 2.085 mm above that tangent point, while the rigid contact bottom stood at 0.650 mm: at least **1.435 mm of geometric overlap** at that sample. This is a baseline collision finding, not a biological measurement. A revision needs its own full-footprint check; a single normal/centroid is insufficient.

## Construction that the physical principles support

A conventional thermoelectric cooler contains P-type and N-type semiconductor legs between electrically insulating ceramic substrates. Patterned conductors join the legs electrically in series while the thermal paths operate in parallel between the two faces. Current alternates through the legs and through interconnects on opposing faces. The ceramic substrates separate the electrical circuit from external plates; they are not an instruction to insert a thick thermal barrier across the useful heat path. [Ferrotec, original manufacturer reference, §2.2](https://thermal.ferrotec.com/technology/thermoelectric-reference-guide/thermalref02/).

The revised illustrative circuit admits a simple topology check: two terminal nodes have one connection each; every intermediate leg/interconnect node belongs to the same continuous path; no bus shorts adjacent couples. Show an electrically series route, not a row of disconnected colored pillars. This check establishes connectivity only. It does not establish contact resistance, solder integrity, module reliability, manufacturability or thermoelectric coefficients.

Rigid TEC mounting faces and tissue contact are different interfaces. Conventional module installation requires attention to flat mating surfaces, thermal contact, mechanical loading and differential expansion; a cooler should not serve as an arbitrary structural support. Those manufacturer requirements do **not** provide permissible brain contact pressure, implant fixation loads, biocompatible adhesives, or surgical instructions. [Ferrotec, §6.1–6.2](https://thermal.ferrotec.com/technology/thermoelectric-reference-guide/thermalref06/). A flexible tissue-facing layer, ceramic electrical isolation and lateral thermal insulation have different purposes; their thicknesses and properties remain unselected.

The hot side receives `Qh = Qc + P`: extracted heat plus electrical input. A spreader redistributes heat but does not destroy it. The laboratory's finite hot-node storage and finite resistance to a fixed 37 °C reservoir remain explicit boundary assumptions; the rendering supplies no measured route to that reservoir. [Ferrotec, §5.1](https://thermal.ferrotec.com/technology/thermoelectric-reference-guide/thermalref05/) explains the requirement for a sink; [§11.4](https://thermal.ferrotec.com/technology/thermoelectric-reference-guide/thermalref11/) supplies the balance. No implanted fin, liquid loop, battery, remote package or chronic rejection system is established by drawing one.

## Packaging is a boundary, not a finish

Implant electronics need protection from body fluids and electrically isolated connections across that protection. Manufacturer examples distinguish enclosure walls, closure, feedthrough conductors and insulating seals. They also distinguish silicone overmolding from hermetic packaging. See [SCHOTT's implant enclosure/feedthrough technical information](https://www.schott.com/en-us/products/customized-enclosures-and-feedthroughs-for-implantable-medical-devices-p1001211/technical-details) and [CorTec's packaging and subassemblies description](https://cortec-neuro.com/cdmo/hermetic-packaging). These are engineering precedents, not selected SeizeFreeze suppliers, partnerships or proof that the proposed thermal window can be sealed.

A cutaway may expose semiconductor legs, PCB traces and internal spreaders for teaching. It must show that a wall has been removed from the view, rather than depict those internals as directly exposed in a finished implant. A housing closure seam, feedthrough and thermal window may be presented as **candidate interfaces requiring design and testing**. An attractive seam does not establish a leak rate, corrosion resistance, electrical isolation, sterilization compatibility or lifetime. Screws drawn for an open demonstrator do not establish a hermetic lid closure or cranial fixation method.

## Keep numerical and physical claims separate

- The solver assumes a 100 mm² uniform thermal footprint. A displayed 10 × 10 mm span describes an assumed scale; it does not demonstrate full-area cortical contact or characterize fluid layers, pressure, meningeal placement or vessels.
- The module's assumed Seebeck coefficient, resistance and conductance are lumped parameters. They are not calculated from the rendered leg dimensions or couple count.
- Cold/hot thermal capacities of 0.8 and 6 J/K are model assumptions, not masses derived from the rendered package. PCB, driver and sensing power remain outside the quantified TEC power budget.
- Tissue-side sensing in the model observes the reconstructed contact surface through an assumed lag. Any additional sensor geometry is a proposed measurement location, not a validated sensor suite.
- Position above intact dura versus nearer the pial surface remains unresolved. [Anatomical notes](ANATOMY.md) define the distinction. The final scene must retain the actual layers or explicitly identify the educational removal; it must not imply a standard implantation procedure.
- Camera cutaways, exploded gaps and material colors are display choices. They do not imply a clinically suitable device, patient-specific placement, effective seizure suppression or a completed product specification.

The [model record](MODEL.md) defines what is calculated; the [evidence map](EVIDENCE.md) defines the project status and external research. A defensible device visualization must agree with both. Establishing actual hardware dimensions and operating behavior would require a selected or fabricated module, measured materials and interfaces, electrical and thermal characterization, packaging experiments and appropriately governed biological work.

Review limit: software source, geometry and rendering checks only. Geometric verification below does not establish manufacturing accuracy, tissue contact, implant safety or independent hardware/clinical validation.


## Reproducible construction checks for the revision

The 6 × 6 serpentine array has 36 alternating N/P legs, 18 cold-face and 17 hot-face bridges, and two hot-face terminals. Its graph contains 74 nodes and 73 edges in one unbranched path. The 18-couple count is illustrative. All cold ceramic, patterned copper, leg, upper copper and hot ceramic heights meet at their defined interfaces; the assembly is authored in millimeters and mounted with unit scale.

The revised rigid footprint is checked against all 315,514 source cortical triangles. The full-footprint maximum is independently verified by edge intersections, including triangles whose vertices lie outside the footprint. The display datum is 2.9991324 mm above the chosen atlas tangent; minimum raw-cortex rigid clearance is 0.3000000 mm. The margin also accommodates the displayed 0.13 mm pia proxy. This is a geometric rendering check, not a pressure, vessel-clearance, surgical or safety specification.

At 0.5 mm sampling, the unresolved datum-to-cortex gap spans approximately 1.11–8.03 mm between reference cortex and flat spreader. The displayed contact envelope uses a separate 24 × 24 grid and a 0.20 mm display offset above the raw source cortex; its displayed thickness range is therefore different. Neither range is a tissue measurement. This exposes a substantial unresolved contact problem. It does not specify a conformable filler, establish tissue loading or imply the solver's assumed 4 K/W contact resistance follows from that shape. The cortex has not been flattened to fit the device. Dimensions and thermal parameters are deliberately not calibrated to one another.
