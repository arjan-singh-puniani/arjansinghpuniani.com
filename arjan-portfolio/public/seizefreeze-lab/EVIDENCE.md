# SeizeFreeze · evidence and development path

Reviewed 30 October 2026. This is an educational exploration of Cyberpunk Reality's proposed closed-loop focal cortical-cooling concept. The interactive model is new project-specific software, not device telemetry or a reproduction of an experimental SeizeFreeze device.

## What the record supports

The portfolio identifies Arjan Singh Puniani as founder and device lead, with the project dating from 2022. The available project artifacts establish a proposed architecture, concept illustrations, an early pitch film, and prototype-planning requirements. No device-specific bench dataset, calibrated device model, animal experiment, human testing record, or clinical validation was found in the inspected project record. Development status therefore remains **concept and prototype planning**.

The [University of Pittsburgh's 2023 Randall winners record, page 2](https://www.bigidea.pitt.edu/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/Meet-the-Randall-2023-Winners-PDF.pdf) names Arjan Singh Puniani, Alex Ezerins, Wendi Yin, and Jack Ketchum for Cyberpunk Reality. The portfolio records a third-place $5,000 Randall award and a $7,500 Kuzneski Innovation Cup award. These are team competition recognitions, not technical validation. The [Pitt Research 2022–23 annual report, printed page 61](https://www.research.pitt.edu/sites/default/files/pitt-research-annual-report-2022-23.pdf) independently lists CyberPunk Reality among winners of both competitions; its extracted text does not allocate the Kuzneski prize amount to individual teams. The existing winner graphic is the amount-specific project source. The supplied record does not establish a detailed allocation of engineering contributions among collaborators.

Patent and regulatory progress are not asserted here. Prior source audits reported conflicting IP wording; no current authoritative filing or grant record was verified. Absence of a record in this review is not proof of absence. No regulatory-clearance claim is supported by the inspected record.

## Simulation, uncertainty, and biological separation

The solver implements an educational coupled thermal model. Every numerical material property, contact area, conductance, thermal mass, sensor lag, detector setting, current limit, shutdown bound and controller target must be read with the model documentation. Unless a source and measurement context explicitly say otherwise, these values are **exploratory assumptions**. A displayed interlock is an illustrative software behavior, not a clinically established protection limit.

The tissue field is a simplified computational domain. The visual cortical surface is anatomical illustration, not a patient scan. A reduced field displayed on it cannot establish patient-specific cooling reach. Model temperatures and energy are simulated; the electrophysiological signal and any sensor noise are synthetic. A spatial threshold or cooled-region metric describes a chosen numerical threshold only, never a therapeutic volume.

Neural event generation remains independent of cooling. Neither current activation nor a colder field causes the synthetic waveform to recover automatically. Detection, control action, heat transfer and hypothesized biological benefit are separate propositions. There is no validated temperature-to-seizure-suppression relationship in this experience.

Sensitivity controls expose dependencies rather than confidence intervals. A varied assumption is not a measured distribution, and an envelope over selected inputs is not a probability of patient benefit. Model verification and source checks are computational work, not independent expert review.

## Development path: experiments that could reduce uncertainty

1. **Instrumented thermal bench:** measure interface temperature, spatial gradients, cold-side extraction, hot-side accumulation and input power on a known phantom. Calibrate sensor lag and uncertainty; fit module and contact parameters. Compare the model against held-out thermal transients rather than only fitting one run.
2. **Heat rejection and duty cycle:** measure continuous and repeated-event behavior with a physically specified sink. Check where every joule goes and how package temperature recovers. Establish whether the intended footprint and power budget can reject heat without unacceptable surrounding warming; no acceptance limit is claimed here.
3. **Contact and packaging:** test realistic interfaces, fluid exposure, seal/feedthrough integrity, electrical isolation, thermal cycling, fixation, sterilization compatibility and fault behavior. Characterize manufacturing tolerances and inspectable failure criteria.
4. **Detection and control:** use appropriately governed, labeled electrophysiological datasets to quantify false alarms, missed events, detection latency and artifact response. Separate detector evaluation from thermal-actuator evaluation. Test sensor loss, saturation and independent shutdown on hardware.
5. **Preclinical and translational program:** only with relevant review and approvals, investigate spatial reach, functional effects, repeated exposure and chronic tissue response in an appropriate model. Human feasibility, clinical benefit, regulatory pathway and manufacturing scale would each require their own evidence and specialist input.

These are proposed milestones, not completed studies, scheduled trials, commercial forecasts, or regulatory commitments.
