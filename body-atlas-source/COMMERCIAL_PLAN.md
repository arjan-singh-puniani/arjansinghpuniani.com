# BODY//ATLAS commercial plan

Decision date: November 15, 2026. Owner: Arjan Singh Puniani.

## Decision

Sell one fixed-scope U.S. neurodevice evidence brief to the founder or R&D lead of an early-stage neurotechnology company preparing an internal design, diligence, or financing discussion. Test $1,500 USD per pilot. Deliver a sourced comparison of up to three agreed devices or applications, an evidence and gaps register, and a 30-minute handoff. The existing public Atlas, tremor example and downloadable comparison remain free. The paid value is a custom answer with claims traced to primary sources and a documented time cutoff.

The founder’s chemical engineering, neural engineering and BCI research background is relevant to explaining mechanisms and tracing claims. The founder’s software work makes the evidence inspectable. Regulatory or clinical advice requires a separate qualified review and sits outside the pilot. The most serious failure mode is that a buyer can compile the same brief with FDA, an existing adviser and a spreadsheet faster than they can onboard an unfamiliar vendor. The immediate milestone is one collected pilot payment against a signed scope, followed by delivery and a recorded buyer assessment.

## Fulfillment, privacy and quality

1. Classify the email as inquiry only. Reply within two business days after activation. Record a lead ID, date, fit, stage and next action in a founder-controlled system. Tell buyers to use public information only. Reject patient identifiers, private technical files and requests for clinical/regulatory recommendations.
2. Agree in writing on one question, U.S. scope, up to three exact applications/devices, research cutoff, deliverables, ten-business-day target, correction round and customer owner. Approval and commercial terms precede work. Quote $1,500 once. Proposed invoice terms are 50% at kickoff and 50% on delivery, reconciliation against settled records only. Use a founder-approved invoice and verified payment destination. If deposits conflict with procurement, agree a PO and due date before booking capacity. Never send payment instructions from a preview.
3. Record searched databases, exact IDs and queries, decision and retrieval dates, record versions, relevant official supplements and document locators. Verify links on access and delivery day. Distinguish original vs later decisions, historical label vs unknown current revision, application vs family vs model/lot, and registry vs outcomes. If lookup fails, mark access failure and date. Never record “no recall” from an incomplete search. Preserve provisional context, exact source scope and any disagreement. Use existing Atlas output as a starting point and work from sources per contract.
4. Package a dated Markdown/PDF brief using the sample structure and a CSV source log. Each row includes assertion ID, source title, canonical URL, application/model ID, decision date, retrieval date, page/section, evidence type, review state, and limitations. Tag regional renderings as reference anatomy. Before sending, trace every material claim to a source locator, open every delivered link and verify the top-line answers against the scope. For the first three paid pilots, budget a separate qualified reviewer for up to two hours to spot-check each brief and log what was reviewed. If unresolved issues affect the conclusion, narrow the answer, pause or refund under signed terms.
5. Send from the approved account to the agreed recipient, offer one 30-minute walkthrough and one factual correction round within 10 business days after delivery. Preserve version and correction log; send a new version on correction. Proposed refund terms for review: refund unearned work if canceled, offer correction or refund if the agreed critical deliverable cannot be met. Founder and counsel approve terms before use. Record payment, delay, hours, revision and 30-day outcome separately.

For a dry run, use the two public PMAs in the example. Reconcile the original 1997-07-31 and 2016-07-11 decision dates with the direct FDA records. Inspect [Activa supplement S007](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf/p960009s007b.pdf) separately from the original and mark current model labeling and recall scope unresolved. Acceptance means the exported file contains both exact original IDs, linkage and limitations, and the sample does not turn a shared thalamic target into an outcome comparison. Use [the worksheet](PILOT_OPERATIONS.md) to rehearse quote, collection and release without emailing or invoicing.

Review active cases at intake and delivery. Monthly run the existing bounded ingestion and compare proposed diffs, failed jobs, freshness and checksums before approving any snapshot refresh. Keep source snapshots immutable, log outages, and show failed collections with their timestamps. There is no verified unattended hosted ingestion or complete coverage. No accounts are needed. Static pilot traffic uses existing hosting. Remote APIs remain limited to their existing functions. Confirm deployment monitoring and an error owner before launch. Retain contact emails only as needed for follow-up and business records, restrict mailbox/access, and agree a retention policy before launch. Proposed duration: 90 days for unqualified inquiries, subject to the founder's privacy review and any contractual/legal requirements.

Assumes each modeled paid contract is also delivered and fully collected by day 90, with no refunds or tax. A 50% deposit alone contributes $750 cash and does not make a second paying customer.

Formulas. Revenue = count × $1,500. Direct cost/pilot = 9 founder hours × $75 ($675, including 1.5 hours for support, handoff and correction) + 2 external review hours × $100 ($200) + $15 assumed payment cost (1% blended budget, verify provider) + $30 for preparation/incidentals = $920. Gross margin = ($1,500 − $920) ÷ $1,500 = 38.7%. Acquisition expense = founder hours (14/26/38) × $75 plus cash budget ($30/$60/$120). Fixed = $90 infrastructure plus 4 refresh/admin hours × $75. Operating result = gross profit − acquisition − fixed; excludes taxes, prior development and one-off professional sign-off. If paid review is higher or no reviewer can be secured within scope, reprice or decline.

Base cash cost excluding imputed founder time = $490 direct + $60 acquisition + $90 infrastructure = $640, before one-off terms/licensing review. All-in economics are negative in all discovery scenarios. At base launch overhead, 5 pilots at $580 gross contribution cover $2,400 overhead. Base modeled acquisition cost is $1,005/pilot, above $580 contribution, so scaling the initial channel would multiply the loss. Reuse a direct intro and referenceable sample before attempting to scale. At 2 pilots/month with $130 monthly fixed expense, acquisition must fall below $515/pilot for positive modeled operating profit. Target ≤$350/pilot after discovery, with evidence from actual hours/receipts.

Sensitivity from base. Proposal conversion at 25% yields 1 paid $1,500 pilot and −$1,820. If both payments slip past day 90, cash collected in-window becomes $0 (or $1,500 if both deposits settled); accrued delivery costs and unpaid invoices stay visible. 50% higher acquisition expense yields −$2,245. Review at $400 instead of $200 per pilot yields −$1,640. A 13-hour founder delivery instead of 9 hours yields −$1,840. A fifth paid pilot requires capacity and a genuine conversion improvement.

Monthly collected milestones of $3k/$6k/$9k require 2/4/6 fully collected pilots in that month. Annual receipts of $18k/$36k/$72k require 12/24/48 fully collected pilots a year. At four monthly pilots, delivery alone requires 36 founder hours plus sales and upkeep, and eight outside review hours. Current booking cap is two simultaneous pilots. Base assumes one purchase per customer during 90 days and a 2–6 week sales cycle; repeat revenue is upside only. All modeled revenue is one-time. MRR is $0 in every scenario. Annual contract value is inapplicable until a recurring contract exists.

Three inexpensive falsification tests. **Pain and access**: screen 20 and run five discovery calls; three recent matching workflows or revisit segment. **Willingness to pay**: send four approved $1,500 scoped offers after qualified calls; zero accepted after decision dates means change scope/price or segment. **Deliverability**: time a dry run on two exact PMAs and have a qualified reviewer check the claims; any material citation error or >9 founder hours flags a capacity/quality problem before accepting work.
