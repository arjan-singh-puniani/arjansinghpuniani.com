# Performance Lab design

The instrument is a crafted wood-and-metal station by the back court, with a schematic idle display. Its identity, selection proxy, physical footprint, approach spot and completed visit history belong to the existing object system. **Lab** finds the object; **See the movement** makes the player approach, look and point before opening analysis. Nearby free members can notice through the existing visibility/cooldown logic.

The world remains the stage. Analysis presents the same avatar on the same court, with a quiet instrument panel and a shared transport bar. VectorCourt emphasizes court-space history; AnatomyMotion reveals the body rig; Combined connects the two. Captured surfaces keep their original opacity, and restrained mint/gold annotations stay on the actual joint centres through clothing. Slow contact changes replay speed only; reduced motion removes that slowdown. A separate, opt-in exploded view keeps a fixed offset for inspection and resets to registration whenever the lab opens.

The first visit offers a forehand, serve and walking-strip demonstration. Later visits also offer the most recent Championship's bounded 360-sample capture. Each source is labeled **AUTHORED DEMO** or **GAME RECORDING**. The screenshot references inspire synchronized replay, joint selection and chart layout; they are not evidence that this project can measure muscle activation, ergonomic injury risk or ground reaction forces.

One continuous replay pose drives avatar, skeleton, ball, racket sweep, root trail, coverage, contact/bounce markers, measurement and chart cursor. Orbit, native pause/play, speed and scrub controls are usable by mouse, keyboard and touch. Canvas taps select projected joint landmarks; five native focus buttons provide a dependable alternative. Escape and Return restore the previous club view and leave live owners untouched.

The lab freezes live simulation rather than seeking the real player or rewinding saved history. This deliberately allows inspection without altering contacts, reservations, relationships, clock or foot constraints. On exit, normal club time resumes according to the prior pause setting.

Distances use **club units**, because this miniature court is not a metrically faithful full-size court. Knee/elbow flexion equals 180° minus the segment interior angle; upper-arm elevation uses the torso inferior axis; hip focus is explicitly a trunk–thigh angle; ankle focus is landmark height. Zero-length or missing samples abstain. No health score, force, torque, muscle activation, risk rating or diagnosis appears.

Between observed frames the body and annotations are reconstructed together, preserving exact recorded contact frames. Readouts and the chart cursor advance every render frame, and the current motion trail ends at the moving body. Missing intervals remain missing.
