# Rally House — human playtest

Use a fresh browser profile for the first visit, then repeat with an existing save. This is the remaining human gate, not a list of claimed passes.

- [ ] Enter without instructions. Choose a character, walk, and find a tactile object within 30 seconds.
- [ ] Ring the bell, sip tea, pluck strings, turn a journal page and use a placed object. Confirm the action looks connected to the object and a nearby free member can notice.
- [ ] Leave the game alone briefly. People should keep their plans without becoming a crowd around the player.
- [ ] Compare daylight, evening and rain. Warm corners should feel inviting; the room should stay legible.
- [ ] Challenge a free member. Watch the approach, acknowledgment, orbit and descent. Decide whether the camera transformation feels intentional.
- [ ] Move continuously, release, reverse, then hold movement through a stroke. Judge response and foot/body presentation.
- [ ] Follow every part of the ball's flight and bounce. Try at least three rallies without automation.
- [ ] Hear perfect, clean, defensive and frame impacts on speakers and headphones. A miss must have no contact sound. Adjust volume and confirm mute.
- [ ] Deliberately miss, hit a frame and hit a good shot. Decide whether each lost point is understandable.
- [ ] Finish a complete first-to-seven match. Rematch, then return. The prior club view and time controls should return.
- [ ] Repeat with Barbara and another opponent; every competitor should have a visible racket.
- [ ] Exit during the approach, intro, serve and rally. Confirm controls never remain stuck.
- [ ] Play on a physical phone in portrait and landscape; move and swing simultaneously. Rotate during play, switch away, then return.
- [ ] Enable reduced motion. Check that interaction effects still finish and gameplay remains usable.
- [ ] Play for 15–20 minutes. Look for audio accumulation, unexpected slowdowns, save issues and people stuck in a pose.
- [ ] Reload an existing decorated club. Confirm furniture, member memories and avatar are preserved.

Ask an independent player: Was moving pleasurable? Was contact satisfying? Was the ball continuously readable? Did the rally develop a rhythm? Did losses make sense? Did you want another rally? Did you want to touch something else or stay in the club?

Record concrete observations and device/audio setup here. The build should only receive a studio-ready label after those answers are positive in actual human play.
