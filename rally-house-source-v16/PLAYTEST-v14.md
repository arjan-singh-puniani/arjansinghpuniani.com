# Ten-minute spectator playtest

Use a fresh browser profile for the first run. For a second visit, retain the save. Test on the hardware the player actually uses. A software-rendered headless browser cannot answer whether this is fun.

1. Start a fresh club and watch for ten minutes without touching anything. Do not explain the characters or the new scenes first.
2. Afterward ask the tester to name the people they remember, describe what happened in their own words, and say which moment felt funny, awkward, kind or unconvincing.
3. Ask what they expect or want to happen next. Avoid suggesting a desired answer.
4. On the second visit, let them use “Happening now,” coach, build a bench, and ignore events. Observe whether the prompt helps, obstructs, or feels demanding.
5. Let a familiar ritual return. Ask whether it feels like a recognizable habit or the same joke repeated.

Record exact quotes, the scene IDs involved, device/browser, viewport, whether speech was readable, and whether the tester understood who was speaking. Do not substitute activity counts for those answers.

Release gates: people can distinguish the four members; at least two causal moments are recalled without prompting; exchanges remain readable without repeated camera rescue; the player can keep decorating without interruption; returning dialogue acknowledges history; pauses and reloads do not create fake outcomes. Validate save failure, graphics recovery and physical mobile performance separately.

No human feedback was collected during this implementation. The browser sessions in QA-v14.md are automated observation and interaction checks.
