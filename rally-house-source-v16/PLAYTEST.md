# v15 observation, interaction and attachment review

No independent human feedback was collected. These findings come from source inspection, deterministic simulation, real-time browser automation, UI interactions and inspected screenshots. They cannot establish whether a person became attached to the cast.

## What was played

The unchanged baseline was watched for approximately ten real minutes, then exercised in a 1,203.598-second automated mixed session. The frozen baseline mixed session overlapped implementation in a separate project folder. It was not an independent pre-change manual playtest.

The v15 candidate was watched untouched for approximately 610 real seconds. Its last sample at 607.472 seconds recorded 17 completed activities, 13 families, five pairs and seven locations; there were no unexpected errors or cancellations. Quiet-pose member-time was 61.06%, while unreserved idle member-time was 2.51%. These measure motion/activity state, not enjoyment.

The candidate mixed session lasted 1,206.643 seconds: people/history inspection, camera and keyboard selection, four catalog-and-floor placements, weather, coaching menus, saving, reloading and portrait/landscape inspection. It completed 25 activities with no recorded browser errors. Its lesson-selector logic did not await visibility, so the two logged “requests” were only menu visits. The harness is corrected; the raw record is retained unchanged.

A separate real-time UI coaching check confirmed actual selection and completion. Timing practice took Mika from 28 to 33; a queued movement lesson took her to 36. Both completed eight contacts. Closing time and a busy coach were staged for the second case. One earlier attempt timed out; an instrumented rerun completed in 100.586 seconds, and ten timing variations passed in the deterministic Game harness. The timeout's root cause remains unestablished.

## Building findings

The mixed session placed a bench at (-5,-7), plant at (-3,-7), basket at (-1,-7) and lamp at (1,-7). The bench accumulated six completed two-person visits involving all four members. Nia, June and Leo adopted it as a favorite; Mika had two visits and had not yet done so. The other three placed objects had no visits in that particular session. They must not be described as used merely because they were built.

The separate 3,600-second built-club simulation did show Nia adopting a plant and Mika/Leo adopting a basket, with all four favoring its bench. Different layout/time/context produces different outcomes. The browser favorite-pose check completed actual visits before examining the resulting shared-bench scene.

## Changes prompted by review

A route sweep exposed blocked entrance/lobby access. Close-up screenshots caught dialogue beats repeatedly restarting the sitting pose. Source/causal review caught premature intention consumption, missing witness memory and insufficient saved-field validation. The closing queue was checked separately from the flawed mixed-session menu actions.

The long mixed session also revealed that opening-hours gating silenced every autonomous scene after closing. The packaged version keeps a slower rhythm of reflection, rest, care and shared breaks after hours. A 300-second deterministic case produces four quiet activities; targeted real-time browser observation is recorded in `qa/v15-night-browser.json`. The full twenty-minute candidate session was not repeated after that fix.

## Earnest critique

The cast is more distinguishable in choice and remembered consequence. The strongest evidence is the witnessed-game chain and the player-built bench gaining regulars. Shared breaks now have different initiators and relevant destinations rather than relying on Nia to start everything.

The art still limits close-up acting. Hands, cups, sitting joints and facial primitives need an artist-led motion pass. Some dialogue will become familiar quickly despite the combinatorial setup. A smaller number of excellent additional reactions may help more than adding systems. Wide camera views still make subtle behavior easy to miss.

After-hours life is intentionally quieter and is not a home/sleep simulation. Long-lived culture values, major-memory growth and unusual dense layouts need further testing. The one unresolved browser coaching timeout prevents an unconditional reliability claim. Headless mobile emulation does not establish physical-phone performance.

## Independent human release gate

Recruit players without explaining the cast or desired stories. Give them a twenty-to-thirty-minute session with watch, build and coach opportunities, then ask:

1. Can you name the four people and describe each personality?
2. What did someone do that you expected? What surprised you?
3. Did you notice a favorite place or a relationship?
4. Did anyone react to something that happened earlier? What was the connection?
5. Did anything you built matter?
6. What would you like to happen tomorrow?
7. If one person disappeared tomorrow, whose absence would you notice?

Record exact answers, device/browser, missed captions, repeated jokes and moments of confusion. Do not count scene totals as answers. Repeat on another day with the same save and on physical mobile hardware. This release has not passed that attachment gate.
