Rituals are candidates rather than appointments that override the club. A saved day stamp reduces repeated same-day selection. State, availability and recent scenes can delay or replace a ritual; weather and time add small biases. Existing schedules still provide the larger daily rhythm.

Five persistent identity anchors are included: June's notebook and cup, Mika's racket, Leo's racket and Nia's watering can. Completed relevant rituals update their use count and last-use place. Visible props attach to their owner during performance. This is not a dropped-item inventory: a racket is not physically left on a bench for later retrieval.

Quiet life avoids pretend coaching on an empty court. Notes, gear inspection, a cup, gaze and occasional context-driven notices fill waiting moments. These do not award lesson progress. Simulation tests assert that rituals cannot seize someone committed to a match or lesson.

After hours, quiet composed rest, reflection, care and company remain eligible at a slower cadence (at least 75 seconds between starts). Autonomous court bookings remain closed; explicit queued lessons retain priority. This is a persistent miniature club rather than a full sleep/home simulation.
